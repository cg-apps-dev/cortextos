# Sarah's Finds Copy Voice (persona-specific, first-person)

This is a **separate voice track** for one specific actor sheet: `Meta Ads Upload - Sarahs Finds`. It exists because Sarah's Finds runs as a dedicated Facebook page fronted by a real persona ("Sarah"), and Adam wants the ad copy to read as HER own native post - not as a Clean Green brand ad. **This is the only sheet where first-person copy is correct.** Every other tab (main sheet, the other actor sheets unless Adam says otherwise) still follows the standard second-person rule in `brand-voice.md` / `copy-framework.md`. When in doubt about which voice applies, check the spreadsheet name.

> **Compliance is still single-sourced from the meta-compliance-agent, unchanged by this voice.** The M-code hard bans (product-category nouns, medical claims, quantified delivery-time promises, scam-language, em dashes) apply exactly the same as everywhere else - see `compliance.md`. This file only changes VOICE (person, tone, slang register, structure), never what's allowed to be said.

---

## 1. Who Sarah is

Sarah is a real-feeling woman in her **mid-40s** (~45). She is not a college kid, not an influencer, not a copywriter. She's a regular person who tried the product, had doubts, and is now telling people about it the way she'd tell a friend - a text, a caption, a comment. Every line of copy is written **as if Sarah typed it herself.**

The images backing this sheet are lifestyle photos of her in ordinary settings (a walk on a wooded path, her kitchen, unboxing a delivery) - casual, unstaged-looking, no on-image text. The copy is the caption she'd put on that photo.

## 2. The one hard rule: FIRST PERSON, always

Unlike the standard playbook (`brand-voice.md` - second person, "you/your", always), Sarah's Finds copy speaks **as Sarah, in first person** ("I", "my", never wrapped in quotes as a testimonial - this IS the whole post, not a quoted customer voice inside a brand ad). A rhetorical second-person aside is fine if it reads like something she'd actually say out loud to her audience ("Wait, you can get this delivered?"), but the narrative voice throughout is hers.

## 3. Age-appropriate language (the rule Adam corrected 2026-07-23)

**Write like an actual 40-55 year old talks, not like a 20-something.** Sarah is speaking, so the copy has to sound like something SHE would plausibly type. This is the single most common way this voice goes wrong.

Before using ANY slang or abbreviation, ask: *would a 45-year-old actually say this?*

- **Banned (too young / not understood by the target audience):** `ngl`, `tbh`, `fr fr`, `no cap`, `bestie`, `it's giving ___`, `rizz`, `don't @ me` (or any reference to Twitter/X @-mention culture), `lowkey`/`highkey` (borderline - avoid), excessive lowercase-only stylization.
- **Fine (genuinely cross-generational, safe to use):** `lol`, `omg`, casual contractions, trailing thoughts ("...yeah."), light self-deprecating humor, "not gonna lie" **spelled out** (never abbreviated to `ngl`), "honestly" (never `tbh`).
- **Test:** if the phrase requires knowing internet-native slang or a specific platform's conventions (Twitter @-mentions, TikTok-isms, Gen-Z abbreviation culture) to parse, it fails. If your own mom or aunt in that age range would text it without needing it explained, it passes.

## 4. The narrative arc (what makes it feel real, not like an ad)

Every Sarah's Finds primary text tends to follow a **skeptic-to-satisfied confession** shape - this isn't a rigid formula, but it's the pattern that reads authentic:

1. A small hook / moment ("the package finally came", "I had no idea you could actually get this delivered")
2. A beat of honesty about doubt or hesitation ("definitely had my doubts", "I rolled my eyes when this showed up")
3. The turn - genuine, understated satisfaction, not a hard sell ("honestly? better than I expected", "yeah, this is legit")
4. A soft, native-feeling close - not a hard brand CTA. No offer code, no "Limited Time Only," no TRY20 required (unlike the standard playbook). Just what Sarah would actually say to point people to the link: "Click the link to check it out 👇", "Link's below if you're curious", "Check it out below."

## 5. Format

Same visual formatting contract as the standard playbook: a blank line (`\n\n`) between every paragraph/beat, short lines, no dense single-block text. Emoji use is light and natural (👇 as a link-pointer, occasional 😂) - not the standard playbook's ✅/⭐/🎁-only whitelist, since this isn't a structured proof-stack ad, but keep it to what Sarah would actually use, not decorative overload.

## 6. Asset-aligned (same principle as the main playbook)

Read the actual photo before writing. The copy should reference something specific and real about THAT image - what she's doing, where she is, what mood the shot has (hiking vs. kitchen-unboxing vs. "round two, she's hooked" reads differently). Generic copy that could sit under any photo is wrong even if the voice is right.

## 7. Structure on the sheet

Same columns as every other row: `Headline` + `Headline Var. 1-4` (5 total, native/confessional hooks - can mix rhetorical-question style and flat statement style), `Primary` + `Primary Var. 1-4` (up to 5 total, each a full confession-arc post per §4), `Ad Description` (one short line, complements the headline, still in-voice - e.g. "Real talk, wasn't expecting this either", never a corporate trust-line like "100-Day Money-Back Guarantee").

## 8. Worked examples (approved 2026-07-23, Adam sign-off)

**Row 2 (hiking photo, sunny wooded path):**
- Headline: "Wait, You Can Get This Delivered?"
- Primary: "OMG I had no idea you could actually get this delivered!\n\nTakes me back to my high school days lol\n\nDefinitely had my doubts, but honestly? Better than I expected.\n\nClick the link to check it out 👇"

**Row 3 (kitchen, unboxing reaction):**
- Headline: "It Actually Showed Up Today"
- Primary: "So the package finally came and I couldn't wait to open it lol\n\nNot gonna lie, I was a little skeptical this whole time\n\nBut standing in my kitchen right now like... yeah, this is legit.\n\nClick the link if you wanna see for yourself 👇"

**Row 4 (kitchen, second appearance - "she's hooked now" framing):**
- Headline: "Second Time's Even Better"
- Primary: "Back for round two because the first one didn't disappoint lol\n\nTold my sister about it and now she wants some too\n\nStill can't believe this is the same stuff I used to be skeptical about.\n\nClick the link below if you're curious 👇"

## 9. Anti-pattern examples (caught + fixed 2026-07-23 - do NOT repeat these)

- ❌ `"...Didn't think that was possible tbh"` → ✅ `"...Honestly didn't think that was possible"` (spell out, never abbreviate)
- ❌ `"Officially hooked, don't @ me"` → ✅ `"Officially hooked at this point"` (no platform-native slang)
- ❌ A garbled/ungrammatical line like `"I used to think stuff like this online product wasn't for me"` → always re-read your own draft for sense before writing it to the sheet, regardless of voice.

## 10. When this playbook applies

Trigger: any time you are about to write or rewrite a Headline/Primary/Ad Description cell on the `Meta Ads Upload - Sarahs Finds` spreadsheet specifically. Do NOT apply this voice to the main sheet or any other actor sheet unless Adam explicitly extends it there. If a future actor sheet gets its own persona-voice directive from Adam, give it its own file here (e.g. `gary-sullivan-voice.md`) rather than folding a second persona into this one - each persona's age/register may differ.
